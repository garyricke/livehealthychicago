1
What is high
blood pressure?

High blood pressure (hypertension) means the force of blood pushing against the walls of your arteries is too high.

You can feel fine and still have high blood pressure. Most people have no symptoms at all — checking is the only way to know.

Categories follow American Heart Association guidelines. If your top and bottom numbers fall in two different categories, use the higher one.

A reading has two numbers — the top (systolic) is the pressure when your heart beats, the bottom (diastolic) is the pressure when it rests.

3
What can you do?
Take charge of your health.

Know your blood pressure goal. Ask your provider what number is right for you.

Check your blood pressure regularly. At home, at a pharmacy, or at a screening event.

Move your body. Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity each week.

Eat less sodium. Aim for less than 2,300 mg per day — about one teaspoon of salt.

Take medications as prescribed. Do not stop without talking to your provider.

Talk to your provider about side effects. There is almost always another option.

Keep your appointments. Follow-up is how progress gets measured.

How to check your
blood pressure
Follow these steps for an accurate reading.
Before measuring
Rest quietly for 5 minutes
Avoid caffeine for 30 minutes
Empty your bladder
Sit with feet flat on the floor and legs uncrossed
During measurement
Use the correct cuff size
Keep your arm supported at heart level
Do not talk or move
Take two readings, 1 minute apart
After measuring
Write your numbers down
Compare them to your goal
Share the results with your provider
