Talking about
high blood pressure
Start the conversation. Build trust. Keep it simple.

Ways to open

  • "Has anyone ever told you your blood pressure was high?"
  • "Do you know your blood pressure numbers?"
  • "When was the last time you had it checked?"

Listen more than you talk.

Explain it simply

HEART = PUMP Your heart pumps the blood.
VESSELS = PIPES Blood travels through the pipes.
HIGH PRESSURE Too much pressure inside the pipes can damage the body over time.

Use plain language

Instead ofTry saying
HypertensionHigh blood pressure
AntihypertensiveMedicine for your blood pressure
ComplianceTaking your medicine every day
DietThe foods you eat
Side effectsProblems or changes you notice

Check understanding

Ask them to explain it back to you:

  • "What is blood pressure?"
  • "Why does checking it matter?"
  • "What can help keep you in a healthy range?"
Red flags — get help right away

High blood pressure usually has no symptoms — someone can feel completely fine.

If a reading is 180/120 or higher:

NO symptoms

Repeat the reading after 1 minute. If it stays high, encourage prompt medical evaluation.
WITH symptoms

Call 911, or follow your organization's policy.

Coaching healthy
lifestyle choices
Small changes. Real life. Big impact.

Healthy eating

  • More fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins.
  • Use herbs and spices instead of salt.
  • Read labels — choose the lower-sodium option.
  • Drink more water.
  • Cook at home when it's possible.

Physical activity

  • Any movement counts.
  • Walk, dance, take the stairs, or try chair exercises.
  • Start small — even 10 minutes makes a difference.
  • Pick something they actually enjoy.

Manage stress

  • Deep breathing, prayer, meditation, or music.
  • Stay connected with family and friends.
  • Take time for yourself.

Medications

  • Take them exactly as prescribed.
  • Never stop without talking to the provider first.
  • Report side effects — there is usually another option.
  • Use reminders, pill boxes, or phone alarms.

Set small, real goals

A good goal is SMART:

Specific Relevant
Measurable Time-bound
Achievable

"I'll walk 10 minutes after dinner, three nights this week."

Coaching pearl

Encourage. Empower. Celebrate progress. You are their partner, not their boss.
